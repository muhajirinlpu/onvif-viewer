# Tuya HLS elapsed-drift guard (not a capture clock)

The observed incident was an advancing Tuya HLS feed with footage about ten minutes behind current vendor-app footage. A fresh browser still showed 15–19 seconds behind the *HLS edge*, so rebuilding the player did not account for the stale edge. A targeted Tuya reconnect reduced the visible delay to about eight seconds. This proves recovery worked in that instance; the original queue/timebase cause is **not proven**. In particular, the fixed `-itsscale:v 1.50` is an approximation of a variable delivered frame rate, but this observation alone does not establish it as the cause. No input retime or audio flags were changed here.

The new `/api/stream/list` `freshness` object for Tuya reports `kind: "hls_elapsed_drift"`, `measuredAt`, `wallElapsedSeconds`, `mediaElapsedSeconds`, and `driftSeconds` (media minus wall). The sample compares successive HLS media-sequence windows' `#EXTINF` durations against wall-clock elapsed time for **one FFmpeg run**. A window is considered only when all referenced local segment files exist; the parser reads no video content and does not probe RTSP. It is omitted until an advancing comparison exists and removed when timing is untrustworthy. It is neither source capture age nor viewer latency; HLS program-date-time, segment filename, and filesystem modification time are not treated as camera capture timestamps. An upstream queue could be stale while the measured rate stays near zero drift, so this guard cannot certify footage is current.

Tuya-only recovery requires at least three minutes in a run, drift at least 120 seconds across three advancing samples, and ten minutes between automatic interventions. A long source gap, missing segments in the observed window, bad/zero duration, sequence reset, or explicit discontinuity invalidates the baseline instead of triggering recovery. A newly started FFmpeg run takes a fresh baseline; the cooldown survives the run. The watchdog checks exact run/command ownership and suspension before signalling its own process group. The existing monitor does the ordinary Wait/reconnect and avoids a second media probe for the Tuya loopback bridge. ONVIF behavior and the 30-second Tuya input I/O timeout are unchanged. Automatic restarts are a bounded safety response to *measured advancing media-vs-wall divergence*, not a demonstrated cure of the upstream cause or a guarantee of fresh imagery.

Synthetic loop: `GOMAXPROCS=2 GOFLAGS=-p=1 GOTMPDIR="$TMPDIR" go test ./internal/stream -run 'TestAdvancingHLSCanAccumulateDriftAndRecoverOnlyTuya|TestFreshness' -count=1`. It advances five-entry synthetic playlists by one sequence every six wall seconds while declaring seven media seconds; the former progress-only watchdog sees activity, while elapsed drift grows until the guard trips. Steady cadence, gap, discontinuity, reset, cooldown, invalid duration, ownership, ONVIF exclusion, and telemetry are covered. No live camera, extra P2P producer, service, or binary is involved.

Remaining validation before deployment: independently review playlist-duration semantics against the deployed FFmpeg output, then observe real telemetry and compare it with separately verified frame-clock footage age over a long run. If the feed goes stale at zero rate drift, investigate bridge/source buffering and do not claim this guard solved it. No deployment was performed.
